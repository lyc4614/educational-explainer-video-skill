# Explanation design, asset quality, and motion variety

Use this guide before generating or selecting layered assets, and when a technically valid video still feels repetitive or weak. It extends the existing brief and asset plan; it does not change their schemas or the A/B/C approval rules.

## Start with an observable explanation

Plan in this order:

source passage → viewer's question → relationship → visible action and before/after states → required subjects and independent layers → cue events → asset selection/generation → composition.

For each visual paragraph, write a project-local row containing the source cue IDs/time envelope, takeaway, explanatory action, before/after states, needed layers or poses, and the exact cue event that permits the result. Put these details in the existing `stateChanges`, `enterState`, `exitState`, and companion `asset-plan.json`; do not add fields to the closed production-brief schema.

Apply the action-capability check to each proposed reuse: what must visibly happen, what can this actual asset do, and what is missing? A school image can identify a destination. It cannot explain a cost accumulation or an eligibility decision merely by appearing beside text. An empty generation queue is justified only when the needed actions and quality are covered, not when enough topic-related images exist.

## Evaluate reuse on three separate grounds

1. **Semantic fit:** the depicted subject and pose support the specific action, not only the broad topic.
2. **Visual quality at delivery scale:** inspect silhouette, anatomy/hands where relevant, proportions, prop construction, materials, lighting, edges and unwanted marks. Judge on the actual background at intended size, including a phone-size view. Alpha/bounds checks do not establish these qualities.
3. **Sequence contribution:** inspect adjacent shots. Repeating the same pose, campus or left-subject/right-card layout may add no new understanding, even when each individual asset is relevant.

Reuse suitable assets; refine a salvageable one or generate a missing action state when those checks expose a real gap. Do not require every image to be new, a fixed asset count, a reuse percentage, or a new camera angle every few seconds. A deliberate readable hold is valid. Diversity must come from the manuscript's different relationships, not decorative substitutions.

## Brief assets for the motion they must support

A useful asset brief identifies the source phrase, explanatory role, required action, starting/ending pose, intended on-screen size and orientation, independent parts, contact/pivot points, and style reference. Keep labels and factual values editable.

If the action is handing over a document, choose an appropriate hand/prop state, a small pose sequence, or a rig with usable pivots. Do not slide a raised-hand figure across the screen and call that a handover or a walk. Generate only the states the shot needs. Keep the same character's face, clothing, scale and lighting consistent between states, and review intermediate silhouettes and prop ownership. If that cannot be made convincing, choose an honest diagrammatic action that explains the same relation.

If the user explicitly requires a raster action subject, do not silently substitute a diagram to avoid that deliverable. Otherwise choose the representation that best explains the source within the authorised scope.

For a familiar 3D style, retain the approved material/light language while varying roles and situations: choosing, comparing, checking a document, allocating resources, reviewing, or practising. Changing clothing colour on the same pose does not supply a missing action. A new beautiful render with no explanatory job is still unnecessary.

## Choose a relationship operator before an entrance effect

| Relationship | Meaningful visible change | Common weak substitute |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Costs accumulate across periods | Identify components and recurrence; move each valid contribution into a retained total/period ledger | Several fee cards fade in without showing what adds up |
| Resources are allocated | Establish the whole, separate the supported share, then route it to recipients | An isolated percentage beside a campus |
| Conditional progression | Reach the checkpoint, wait, show the stated condition, then reveal the conditional continuation | A straight arrow that reads as guaranteed success |
| Distinct eligibility checks | Carry one record through separate checks; passing one does not clear the next | One universal green check |
| Relative position | Keep the same person/value while the comparison group changes | Replace one unrelated student with another |
| Find the failed step | Show the sequence, stop at the relevant node and localise the problem | A generic question mark or floating book |

Do not turn every relation into a gate, a line, or a card. Compare candidates by what the viewer can infer from the action. Use entrance, docking, easing, anticipation and follow-through to clarify that primary action; avoid perpetual floating, identical bounces and decorative camera moves.

## Review time as well as still frames

For the opening and each meaningful passage, note what the viewer learns at successive source events. If only captions, headings, opacity or a slow camera push change while the same poster remains, redesign the explanatory progression. Sample the opening at several moments and watch it forward. A fifteen-second opening check is a useful diagnostic for a long introduction, not a universal shot-length rule.

Review a contact sheet across neighbouring shots for repeated subjects, poses, framing and operators. Hide explanatory cards temporarily: can the action still convey the intended allocation, comparison or condition? Then restore the text and verify units, limitations and reading time. This diagnostic does not require every concept to be understandable without words.

Keep four judgments separate: semantic explanation, asset/art quality, temporal motion/readability, and technical media correctness. Record weaknesses in each; a clean decode, schema, image hash or motion-state test cannot clear the other three. Check the actual exported clip as well as the storyboard.

## Improvement experiments and evidence

For future production, a small set of representative shots can compare a refined action asset, a multi-state subject and a richer relationship operator before investing in a full library. Choose examples from the actual manuscript and existing approval scope; do not impose extra approval loops for already authorised work.

Treat refined art direction, pose sets and comprehension improvements as proposals until actual images and clips have been reviewed. A planning pressure test shows that instructions changed decisions; it does not demonstrate image quality, audience comprehension or a finished video. Preserve current approved outputs unless their revision is requested.
